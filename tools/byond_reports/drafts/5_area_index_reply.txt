Some numbers from inside the game, for context. 516.1688, our tgstation fork on a 255x255x12 map: 780,300 turfs, 243 to 259 areas (some ruins are random). Three runs with 20 player bots; the ranges below cover all three.

Per area, the mean over real station rooms:

for(var/obj/machinery/M in area), 60 rooms, 4,832 machines: 0.43 to 0.44 ms. The same machines from a plain list: 0.01 ms.
for(var/turf/T in area), 131 rooms, about 160 turfs each: 0.34 to 0.37 ms. From a plain list: 0.013 to 0.014 ms.
The machines reached through a plain list of the room's turfs: 0.11 to 0.13 ms. So a turf index would make turf loops about 25 times faster, and loops over the objects in an area about 4 times.
locate(/turf/open) in area: 0.04 to 0.06 ms.
T in area: 0.0001 ms, so membership tests are already fine.

Where it shows up:

Every area calls power_change() from LateInitialize, which loops over the machines in it. That's one scan per area in a single boot tick, about 110 ms of the 250 to 290 ms that tick spends in power_change().
Two boot procs read contents.len and contents[1] for every area: 110 to 140 ms each. We've switched both to area.z, which gives the same answers and costs under 1 ms.
Our player bots read a room's turfs to pick somewhere to walk, 650 to 800 times in 15 minutes with 20 players. We keep our own turf lists for that now.

So we can work around the hot spots, but each workaround is a list we keep in sync ourselves, and anything that changes a turf's area (shuttles, blueprints, map loading) has to know about all of them. That's the reason for asking for the index.
